## 4. How we keep the copies exact

| Step | What we do |
|---|---|
| Copy, not retype | Text comes from the saved page or the pinned data file by script (`content/tools/`). Nobody retypes scripture. |
| Hadith pages | The page is saved from a real browser. The page text is hashed in the browser and again on the saved file, and the two sha256 values must match (`drafts/hadith/provenance-*.json`). |
| Verses | `tools/verify_arabic.py --strict` compares every verse with the pinned KFGQPC file (sha256 above) and fails on any difference. |
| Review ledger | `mark_reviewed.py` writes a sha256 of each reviewed item into `reviewed.json`. If an item changes, its review goes stale and it is stored as `seeded` again. |
| Excerpts are card-only | Tafsir, sirah, aqidah, fiqh and FAQ text goes to the card only. The language model gets the citation and our own explanation, never the source paragraph (commit bd5e7286). |
| Two kinds of text | The card keeps the source's words and our words in separate blocks, with separate labels (ruling f). |

## 5. What we never do

- We never write Quran or hadith from memory, in code, in prompts or in seed data.
- We never read a verse out with text-to-speech. Verses are played as recitation only.
- We never give the language model a source excerpt to quote. It only sees the citation and our explanation.
- We never give a personal fatwa. Fiqh is general information, and we refer the child to a parent or scholar.
- We never use a source that is not in this file. Fatwa sites, modern tafsirs and unlabelled dumps are out.
- We never say a hadith's grade ourselves. The grade is the compiler's, as shown on dorar.net.
- We never edit the English of a translation.
